# TotalRecalls YouTube Launch Plan

Nov 14, 2026 · @Andre

## Channel positioning

The channel should be the place people go to learn how to keep, find, and own their AI conversations, with TotalRecalls shown as the easiest way to do it.

**Who it is for:** heavy users of ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and other assistants who have hundreds or thousands of chats buried in web sidebars. Many already use Obsidian or Notion, and many care about privacy because their chats hold medical, financial, and personal questions.

**One-line promise:** Your AI conversations are your work. Keep them, search them, own them.

**Three content pillars**

1. **How-to and export guides.** One video per provider, aimed at people searching for how to export or download their history. This is where new viewers will find you.
2. **Why ownership matters.** Short explainers on cloud history drift, privacy, backups, and why plain Markdown and JSON files outlast any chat interface.
3. **Workflows and proof.** Obsidian and Notion setups, Smart Search Engine (SSE) demos, and founder stories such as the 8,135-conversations archive.

**Keep in mind:** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11 today, so say that early in videos and point Mac and Linux viewers to your update list. The Free Tier (ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each) is the safest call to action because viewers can try it without paying.

## First 10 videos

Publish in this order: two export how-tos to catch search traffic, then the flagship story, then the rest. Each outline lists a hook for the first 15 seconds, the beats to cover, and a call to action. Check every provider-specific claim on screen while recording, since provider menus and export options change.

## Flagship script: "I saved 8,135 AI conversations"

Target length is 7 to 9 minutes. Anything in \[brackets\] is a placeholder for a real finding or number from your own archive; fill those in before recording, and do not use a number you have not checked. The script is written in first person, as the founder.

**On screen:** a fast scroll through the Library folder, then the file count.

**Say:** "For over two years, I have talked to AI almost every day. ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and more. That adds up to 8,135 conversations. I just saved every one of them onto my own computer, and I want to show you what is in there, and why you should do the same."

### The problem (0:30-1:45)

**On screen:** a long chat sidebar, scrolling endlessly; a browser with many closed tabs.

**Say:** "Here is the issue. These chats are some of the most personal things we write: health questions, money decisions, half-finished ideas. But they live in a sidebar that keeps growing. Old chats sink. Interfaces change. And if you want one specific answer from last year, good luck finding it."

**Say:** "I built TotalRecalls because I wanted these conversations as plain files that I control."

### How it works (1:45-3:30)

**On screen:** the app on Windows; picking providers; the download in progress.

**Say:** "You install the app, sign in to each provider inside the app, and pick what to download. Nothing goes through my servers. Your chats go from the provider straight to your PC."

**Say:** "Each conversation becomes two files: a Markdown file you can read in any editor, and a JSON file with the full structure. They land in one folder, organized by provider."

**On screen:** open one .md file in Notepad, then show the matching .json.

### What I found (3:30-6:00)

**On screen:** Windows Explorer search, charts or folder counts you prepare.

**Say:** "Now the fun part. With everything in one place, I could finally ask questions about my own history."

1. **How much:** \[number of conversations per provider, and how that changed over two years\].
2. **What I asked about most:** \[top topics you find, for example coding, writing, research\].
3. **Something I had forgotten:** \[a specific old chat you rediscovered and what it was worth\].
4. **Something I would have lost:** \[a chat that is hard to reach in the web interface but easy to open as a file\].

**Say:** "None of this was possible while the chats were scattered across eight websites."

### Searching it all (6:00-7:30)

**On screen:** Windows Explorer search first; then Obsidian; then the Smart Search Engine (SSE) with a plain-English question.

**Say:** "Because these are normal files, Windows Explorer, Obsidian, and Notion can already read them. If you want to find a chat by meaning, even when you forget the exact words, that is what the optional Smart Search Engine does."

### Close and call to action (7:30-8:30)

**On screen:** the Microsoft Store page, then the Free Tier download page.

**Say:** "If you want to try this on your own chats, there is a free tier. It covers ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, with five conversations each, so you can see the files before you spend anything. Pro unlocks all eight providers. Links are in the description, and the app is on the Microsoft Store."

**Say:** "Last question for you: how many AI conversations do you think you have? Tell me in the comments, and I will tell you what I would do first."

## Weekly posting workflow

Make one long YouTube video each week and turn it into every other post, so social media adds about two hours of work, not a second job.

1. **Monday:** publish the long video with the description template and pinned comment from the previous section.
2. **Monday:** cut 2 to 3 vertical clips (30 to 60 seconds) and schedule them across Shorts, Reels, and TikTok through the week.
3. **Tuesday:** post a LinkedIn piece on the video's main idea, written for a work audience.
4. **Tuesday to Friday:** post 4 to 5 short X posts: one clip, one tip, one question, one build-in-public update.
5. **Wednesday and Friday:** spend 20 minutes each on Reddit and answer real questions about exporting or searching AI chats. Link your guide only where it truly answers the question.
6. **Friday:** note which post got the most clicks to the Free Tier page and make next week's video on that topic.

Track three numbers weekly: Free Tier downloads, Microsoft Store page visits, and Pro purchases. Add a simple tag to your links, such as a different ending for each channel, so you can see which one is working.
