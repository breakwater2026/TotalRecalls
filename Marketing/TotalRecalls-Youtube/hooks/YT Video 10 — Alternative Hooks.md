# Video 10 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 10 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 10 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry delivery used across the series.

Videos 1 through 9 named the problem, showed the daily friction, proved ownership, protected privacy, put saved chats to work, covered all 8 providers, and told the founder story. Video 10 closes the series. These hooks pull everything together into one clear message: your AI chats belong on your own PC, and now is the time to start. The humor stays dry, but the push toward action is stronger here than in any earlier hook.

## Hook Option 1: The Highlight Reel

**\[VO / Founder, calm and quietly confident]**\
Nine videos. One point.\
**\[pause: one beat, props swept aside]**\
Your AI chats belong on your PC. Here's how to start.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
9 VIDEOS.\
1 POINT.\
YOUR AI CHATS. YOUR PC.

**\[Visual]**\
Wide-ish medium shot of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. The desk is crowded with props from earlier videos: a sticky note, a paper cup, a USB stick, a small recipe box, a puzzle piece, a tiny receipt, and a clipboard. On "Nine videos," the founder surveys the clutter with mild pride. During the pause, the founder sweeps everything to one side with a single forearm pass. Only one plain index card remains in the cleared space. It reads "YOUR PC" in marker. On "Here's how to start," the founder slides the card toward the camera and looks up.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft, jumbled clatter of cardboard, plastic, and paper during the sweep. Silence for half a beat. A single clean card "slide" on the desk on "start," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Returning viewers spot the props and feel rewarded for sticking with the series. New viewers still get the point instantly, because a cluttered desk wiped down to one card is easy to read. The hook states the core message in plain words, which is exactly what a final video should do before it asks for action.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder sliding the props back into the frame one by one and resting a hand beside the "YOUR PC" card, matching the opening shot.

## Hook Option 2: The Excuse List

**\[VO / Founder, dry and methodical]**\
Every excuse, crossed off.\
**\[pause: one beat, last line struck through]**\
Still haven't saved a single chat? Let's fix that. Today.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (styled like a handwritten list, dark ink on lined paper)\
~~NEEDS AN ACCOUNT~~\
~~ANOTHER SUBSCRIPTION~~\
~~ONLY WORKS WITH ONE AI~~\
→ LET'S FIX THAT. TODAY.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding a single sheet of lined notebook paper titled "EXCUSES." Three items are written in plain marker: "Needs an account," "Another subscription," and "Only works with one AI." On "crossed off," the founder strikes through the first two lines in quick strokes. During the pause, the founder slowly draws a line through the third item while holding eye contact with the camera. On "Let's fix that," the founder folds the page in half. On "Today," the founder sets it down and taps it once.

**\[SFX]**\
Two quick marker strikes on the first line. One long, squeaky strike during the pause. A crisp paper fold on "fix that," then a single desk tap on "Today" and a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
By Video 10, the viewer who hasn't acted usually has a reason. This hook names the most common ones and removes them in a few seconds, with each crossed-off line pointing back to a promise proven earlier in the series. "Today" turns a summary into a direct call to act, without leaning on a hard sell.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder unfolding a fresh page with the same three uncrossed excuses and uncapping the marker, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 3: Final Boarding Call

**\[VO / Founder, flat airport-announcer cadence]**\
Final boarding call for everyone still copy-pasting AI chats.\
**\[pause: one beat, chime]**\
Your gate: your own PC.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (styled like a departure board, amber on black)\
FINAL CALL\
PASSENGERS: STILL COPY-PASTING\
GATE: YOUR OWN PC

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding a plain handheld mic like a gate agent. A small, unbranded desk bell sits beside the laptop. The founder delivers the first line in a bored, nasal announcer voice without looking up. During the pause, the founder taps the desk bell once. On "Your gate," the founder points toward the laptop, which shows the TotalRecalls app open on a clean desktop. On "your own PC," the founder lowers the mic and raises one eyebrow at the camera.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft airport PA hum under the first line, with a faint echo on the voice. The classic two-tone boarding chime on the bell tap. The PA hum cuts out on "PC," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
"Final" fits the last video of the series, and a boarding call is a kind of urgency everyone recognizes. It doesn't depend on a countdown or a price deadline, so it stays honest. It also calls back to Video 6's copy-paste montage, so returning viewers get one more small payoff, while the gate point lands the next step: install the app on your own PC.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder raising the mic again and leaning toward it, finger hovering over the desk bell, matching the opening pose.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **State the core promise exactly.** TotalRecalls saves chats to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files. Don't say it "backs up everything automatically," syncs between devices, or keeps chats updated without a download.

- **Excuses must match the product.** In Option 2, each crossed-off line must be true:

  - **"Needs an account":** there's no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
  - **"Another subscription":** Pro is $24 one-time, labeled as the launch price.
  - **"Only works with one AI":** Pro covers 8 providers. The Free Tier covers 3.

- **Don't add excuses you can't fully cross off.** Skip lines like "Too complicated," "Takes too long," or "Works on my Mac." TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11 only, and setup time varies by provider.

- **"Today" is an invitation, not a deadline.** Option 2 and Option 3 create urgency through tone. Don't pair them with fake timers or claim the launch price ends on a specific date unless that date is real and confirmed.

- **Keep the props honest in Option 1.** The swept-aside props are callbacks, not features. Don't imply the product does what each prop joked about, such as sorting chats like a recipe box.

- **Gemini works differently.** If the Scene 2 handoff mentions all 8 providers, remember that Gemini comes through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time, and they need internet.** Opening saved files works offline. Downloading doesn't.

- **Get the tiers right in the CTA.**

  - **Free Tier:** ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each.
  - **Pro:** all 8 providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen), unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Always label the price.** Say "$24 one-time" and label it as the launch price on screen. Update the callback, end card, caption, and pinned comment when the launch price ends.

- **Windows only.** Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Props, lists, and departure-board graphics must be unbranded. Handwritten provider names are fine. Official logos, brand colors, and interface screenshots are not.
- **Check every returning prop.** In Option 1, make sure no prop from an earlier video carries a logo, a real price, or a readable personal detail.
- **Keep the airport generic.** In Option 3, avoid real airline names, airport codes, or recognizable terminal designs. The departure board should be a simple custom graphic.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Use a clean desktop.** When the laptop faces the camera, show only TotalRecalls on a tidy desktop with no personal files, notifications, or account names.

### Pause Timing

- **Make the sweep one motion.** In Option 1, a single, confident forearm pass sells the "clear it all away" moment. Rehearse it so props slide off-frame instead of scattering toward the lens.
- **Slow down the last strike.** In Option 2, the first two lines go fast. The third gets a slow, squeaky strike with eye contact. That change in pace is the joke.
- **Stay flat on the announcement.** In Option 3, keep the announcer voice bored and even. Let the chime ring out fully before "Your gate."
- **Hold the final look.** Every hook ends on the founder facing the camera. Give it a half-beat before the cut so the message lands.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Callback Adjustments

Swap the Scene 6 callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

- **Option 1:** "Still one point." (taps the "YOUR PC" card)
- **Option 2:** "Excuses left: zero." (tosses the folded list off the desk)
- **Option 3:** "Now boarding. Seats available." (one last bell tap)

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Weight action over views.** This video is the series closer, so link clicks, Free Tier downloads, and Pro upgrades matter more than raw retention. Pick the winner based on clicks to totalrecalls.app per 1,000 views.
- **Split returning and new viewers on Option 1.** The prop callbacks reward subscribers. If your analytics allow it, check whether Option 1 wins with returning viewers while Option 2 or 3 wins with cold audiences.
- **Mine objections from Option 2's comments.** Viewers may reply with excuses you didn't list, such as "I'm on a Mac." Pin a short, honest reply, and use the most common objections to plan FAQ updates and future videos.
- **Review the full series.** Line up the winning hook from every video. Look for the style that won most often, whether prop comedy, proof-first demos, price-forward lines, or founder honesty, and make it the default for the next round of content.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Use the winning Video 10 hook as a template for evergreen CTA videos and paid video ads that need a strong, action-focused open.
