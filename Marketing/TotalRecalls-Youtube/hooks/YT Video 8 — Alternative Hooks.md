# Video 8 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 8 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 8 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry delivery used across the series.

Videos 1 through 7 moved from naming the problem to proving ownership, protecting privacy, and putting saved chats to work. Video 8 zooms out to the full picture: all 8 providers in one local Library, and the Pro upgrade that unlocks them. These hooks focus on completeness. The Free Tier gives you a real taste. Pro gives you all of it, for one price, paid once.

## Hook Option 1: The Roll Call

**\[VO / Founder, schoolteacher cadence, played completely flat]**\
ChatGPT. Claude. Perplexity. Gemini. Grok. DeepSeek. Mistral. Qwen.\
**\[pause: one beat, pen click]**\
All present. All on my PC.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (upper third, names tick in one by one with a ✓)\
ROLL CALL: 8/8\
ALL PRESENT.\
ALL ON MY PC.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds, holding a clipboard like a teacher on the first day of school. The founder reads each name in a quick, even rhythm without looking up. On each name, cut briefly to a tight screen shot of the local Library folder in File Explorer, where a matching provider subfolder appears: `chatgpt`, `claude`, `perplexity`, and so on. By "Qwen," all 8 subfolders sit neatly in one Library. During the pause, the founder clicks the pen and ticks the last box. On "All on my PC," the founder looks up at the camera with one eyebrow raised.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft, quick "tick" on each name, rising slightly in pitch. A crisp pen click in the pause. A short school-bell "ding" on "PC," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Eight names in a steady rhythm is hard to scroll past. The list itself becomes the pitch. Viewers who use Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, or Qwen hear their tool called out by name, often for the first time in the series. The growing folder list makes "all 8 providers" something they can see, not just a number.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder flipping the clipboard back to a blank page and uncapping the pen, ready to start roll call again, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 2: The Sample Spoon

**\[VO / Founder, dry, like a deli worker at closing time]**\
Five chats each. That's the free sample.\
**\[pause: one beat, ladle rises]**\
Want the whole pot? Twenty-four dollars. Once.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
FREE: 3 TOOLS · 5 CHATS EACH\
PRO: ALL 8 · UNLIMITED\
$24 ONE-TIME · 50% OFF LAUNCH PRICE

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding up a tiny plastic tasting spoon at eye level. A plain, unbranded soup pot sits beside the laptop. On "free sample," the founder studies the spoon, faintly unimpressed. During the pause, the founder sets the spoon down and lifts a full-size ladle out of the pot with a slow, deliberate flourish. On "Once," the founder rests the ladle against the pot and nods at the camera.

**\[SFX]**\
A tiny plastic "tink" as the spoon is raised. A deep, satisfying ladle "clunk" against the pot rim in the pause. A single register "ding" on "Twenty-four dollars," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
It treats the Free Tier as a real, generous sample rather than a trap, which keeps the upgrade pitch honest and low-pressure. The size contrast between spoon and ladle explains the Pro difference in one image. It also brings the one-time price forward, so value-minded viewers get the key fact in the first 5 seconds.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder setting the ladle back in the pot and picking up the tiny spoon again, matching the opening pose.

## Hook Option 3: The Missing Pieces

**\[VO / Founder, calm and faintly puzzled]**\
Three AI tools saved. Five pieces still missing.\
**\[pause: one beat, pieces slide in]**\
Let's finish the whole picture.

*(13 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
SAVED: 3 OF 8\
MISSING: 5\
→ FINISH THE PICTURE.

**\[Visual]**\
Tight three-quarter shot of the desk. A simple 8-piece jigsaw puzzle sits in front of the laptop. Three pieces are placed and labeled "ChatGPT," "Claude," and "Perplexity" in plain handwriting. Five blank gaps remain, and the missing pieces lie face-down beside the frame. Cut to a medium close-up of the founder, who tilts their head at the gaps. During the pause, the founder slides the last five pieces into place in quick succession. Each is labeled by hand: "Gemini," "Grok," "DeepSeek," "Mistral," and "Qwen." On "whole picture," the founder presses the final piece flat and looks up at the camera.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft cardboard scrape as the founder nudges a loose piece. Five quick, satisfying "snap" sounds in the pause, one per piece. Silence for half a beat after "picture," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
An unfinished puzzle is quietly annoying, and viewers want to see it done. That itch carries them past the first few seconds. The three placed pieces match the Free Tier exactly, so the hook shows what Pro adds without saying "upgrade." Returning viewers also get a callback to Video 3's three-cup shell game, now finished at full scale.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder lifting five pieces back out and setting them face-down, leaving the same three-piece gap from the opening shot.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Name the 8 providers exactly.** ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen. Don't say "every AI tool" or "all your AI chats." Say "8 AI tools" or list them by name.

- **Don't promise future providers.** Unless a new provider is confirmed for the current build, don't imply Pro includes tools beyond these 8.

- **Gemini works differently.** Gemini chats come through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download. It's fine to show a `gemini` subfolder in the Library in Option 1 or a "Gemini" puzzle piece in Option 3. Don't show or imply a one-click Gemini download.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time.** The fast subfolder pop-ins in Option 1 and the quick puzzle snaps in Option 3 are visual shorthand. Don't suggest all 8 providers download in a single click.

- **Viewers sign in to each provider locally.** "No account" means no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers inside the app.

- **Get the tiers right every time.**

  - **Free Tier:** ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each.
  - **Pro:** all 8 providers, unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Always label the price.** Say "$24 one-time" and label it as the 50% off launch price on screen. Update Option 2's on-screen text, the callback, the end card, the caption, and the pinned comment when the launch price ends.

- **Keep the Free Tier framing generous.** Option 2 calls the Free Tier a "sample" to show its size, not to belittle it. Don't imply the Free Tier is broken, limited in quality, or a bait-and-switch.

- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider logos or brand colors.** Provider names may appear in plain handwriting on puzzle pieces and clipboards, or as plain-text folder names in File Explorer. Never show official logos, icons, or interface screenshots.
- **Keep props unbranded.** The soup pot, spoon, ladle, clipboard, and puzzle should carry no manufacturer or company marks facing the camera.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Use a clean demo Library.** In Option 1, the Library folder should show only the 8 provider subfolders, with no personal folders or files nearby. Blur account names, email addresses, and anything personal in the File Explorer address bar or sidebar.
- **Use demo chats only.** If any subfolder opens on screen, show non-sensitive demo files with realistic titles, such as `2026-09-02-pricing-page-draft.md`.

### Pause Timing

- **Keep the roll call even.** In Option 1, read all 8 names in a steady, flat rhythm, about a third of a second each. Speeding up or slowing down breaks the joke. Hold the finished 8-folder view for at least half a second so viewers can count it.
- **Let the ladle rise slowly.** In Option 2, the slow lift is the punchline. A quick grab kills it.
- **Space the puzzle snaps.** In Option 3, give each of the five pieces its own distinct snap. Rushing them together blurs the "five missing" payoff.
- **Hold the beats.** Each hook builds to one reveal: the full roll call, the ladle, or the finished puzzle. Let the pause run slightly longer than feels comfortable.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track upgrade clicks on Option 2.** It leads with price, so it may hold slightly fewer viewers but send more people to totalrecalls.app. Compare link clicks and Pro upgrades alongside retention before you call a winner.
- **Compare Option 2 against Video 4's receipt hook.** Both put the $24 one-time price up front. If both win on clicks, price-forward hooks are a proven lever for the final two videos.
- **Watch provider mentions on Option 1.** Comments like "Finally, someone mentioned DeepSeek" or "Does it do Mistral?" show which lesser-known providers draw interest. Use those signals to pick topics for provider-specific guides.
- **Watch returning viewers on Option 3.** It pays off Video 3's shell game, so it may perform better with subscribers than cold audiences. If your analytics allow it, compare retention for returning and new viewers.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in Videos 9 and 10.
