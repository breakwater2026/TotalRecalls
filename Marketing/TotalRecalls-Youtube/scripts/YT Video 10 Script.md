### Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **State the core promise exactly.** TotalRecalls saves chats to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files. Don't say it "backs up everything automatically," syncs between devices, or keeps chats updated without a download.

- **Excuses must match the product.** In Option 2, each crossed-off line must be true:

  - **"Needs an account":** there's no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
  - **"Another subscription":** Pro is $24 one-time, labeled as the launch price.
  - **"Only works with one AI":** Pro covers 8 providers. The Free Tier covers 3.

- **Don't add excuses you can't fully cross off.** Skip lines like "Too complicated," "Takes too long," or "Works on my Mac." TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11 only, and setup time varies by provider.

- **"Today" is an invitation, not a deadline.** Option 2 and Option 3 create urgency through tone. Don't pair them with fake timers or claim the launch price ends on a specific date unless that date is real and confirmed.

- **Keep the props honest in Option 1.** The swept-aside props are callbacks, not features. Don't imply the product does what each prop joked about, such as sorting chats like a recipe box.

- **Gemini works differently.** If the Scene 2 handoff mentions all 8 providers, remember that Gemini comes through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time, and they need internet.** Opening saved files works offline. Downloading doesn't.

- **Get the tiers right in the CTA.**

  - **Free Tier:** ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each.
  - **Pro:** all 8 providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen), unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Always label the price.** Say "$24 one-time" and label it as the launch price on screen. Update the callback, end card, caption, and pinned comment when the launch price ends.

- **Windows only.** Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Props, lists, and departure-board graphics must be unbranded. Handwritten provider names are fine. Official logos, brand colors, and interface screenshots are not.
- **Check every returning prop.** In Option 1, make sure no prop from an earlier video carries a logo, a real price, or a readable personal detail.
- **Keep the airport generic.** In Option 3, avoid real airline names, airport codes, or recognizable terminal designs. The departure board should be a simple custom graphic.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Use a clean desktop.** When the laptop faces the camera, show only TotalRecalls on a tidy desktop with no personal files, notifications, or account names.

### Pause Timing

- **Make the sweep one motion.** In Option 1, a single, confident forearm pass sells the "clear it all away" moment. Rehearse it so props slide off-frame instead of scattering toward the lens.
- **Slow down the last strike.** In Option 2, the first two lines go fast. The third gets a slow, squeaky strike with eye contact. That change in pace is the joke.
- **Stay flat on the announcement.** In Option 3, keep the announcer voice bored and even. Let the chime ring out fully before "Your gate."
- **Hold the final look.** Every hook ends on the founder facing the camera. Give it a half-beat before the cut so the message lands.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Weight action over views.** This video is the series closer, so link clicks, Free Tier downloads, and Pro upgrades matter more than raw retention. Pick the winner based on clicks to totalrecalls.app per 1,000 views.
- **Split returning and new viewers on Option 1.** The prop callbacks reward subscribers. If your analytics allow it, check whether Option 1 wins with returning viewers while Option 2 or 3 wins with cold audiences.
- **Mine objections from Option 2's comments.** Viewers may reply with excuses you didn't list, such as "I'm on a Mac." Pin a short, honest reply, and use the most common objections to plan FAQ updates and future videos.
- **Review the full series.** Line up the winning hook from every video. Look for the style that won most often, whether prop comedy, proof-first demos, price-forward lines, or founder honesty, and make it the default for the next round of content.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Use the winning Video 10 hook as a template for evergreen CTA videos and paid video ads that need a strong, action-focused open.
