## Production Notes

- **Hook timing:** The first spoken word lands at 00:00. Skip the logo sting, intro music, and fade-in.

- **Delivery:** Treat the objection with respect. The founder should sound glad the viewer asked, never defensive. Pause for a full beat after "I'll turn off Wi-Fi" so viewers register what's about to happen.

- **Signposting:** "First" and "Next" in the VO match the two checklist items from Scene 2. Keep the checklist graphic identical each time it appears so viewers can track progress at a glance.

- **Caption style:** Use bold white text with a subtle dark outline, synced to the VO and placed in the lower third. Proof labels sit as upper-left chips so they never overlap captions.

- **Claim accuracy (critical):** This video lives or dies on trust, so every claim must match the current build exactly. Before recording, confirm:

  - The app makes no connections to any TotalRecalls server during download.
  - Whether license activation, Pro unlocks, or update checks contact a server. If any do, add a short, honest line on screen or in the VO, such as "The only other call is a one-time license check. Your conversations never go with it."
  - Saved files open and search fully with the network off.

- **No staged proof:** Record the Resource Monitor and Wi-Fi-off sequences live, in one continuous take where possible. Avoid jump cuts inside the network view. Technical viewers will notice and question them.

- **Network view labeling:** Replace raw IP addresses and hostnames with a clean "Provider (direct)" overlay. Keep the TotalRecalls process name visible so viewers can see which app made the connection.

- **Provider safety:** Never show a real provider's interface, sign-in page, settings, or logos. Blur the sign-in step. Only the TotalRecalls app, Windows folders, Notepad, and Resource Monitor should appear sharp.

- **Privacy on screen:** Record with a demo account holding only non-sensitive chats. Blur email addresses, account names, network adapter names, and any personal details in file names and previews.

- **Verification prep:** Pick a search keyword that returns one clear, readable result offline. Confirm Windows search has indexed the TotalRecalls folder before you record.

- **Pacing:** Scenes 3 and 4 carry the proof, so keep them unhurried. Hold each key frame (the network row, the "No internet" icon, the opened file) for at least one full second.

- **Continuity:** Match the folder layout, file names, window chrome, and founder setup (wardrobe, lighting, background) used across the TotalRecalls series, especially Videos 4, 5, and 7.

- **Color arc:** Cool and uncertain in Scenes 1–2, warm and clear from Scene 3 onward. Let the grade carry the shift from doubt to confidence.

- **Chapters:** Add the timestamps from the description so skeptical viewers can jump straight to the proof.

- **Cards:** Add one mid-video card at about 00:32 pointing to the explainer (Video 1).

- **End screen:** Include the website link, Video 9 ("Free Tier vs Pro: What You Get for $24"), and a subscribe element.

- **Playlists:** Add the video to "Privacy, Safety & Local-First Explained" and "Start Here: Own Your AI Conversations."

- **CTA consistency:** The line "Windows app — visit from your desktop" must appear exactly as written in the video, description, and pinned comment.

- **Pricing check:** The $24 one-time price reflects the 50%-off launch offer. Update the end card, description, and pinned comment once the launch window closes.

### Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Name the 8 providers exactly.** ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen. Don't say "every AI tool" or "all your AI chats." Say "8 AI tools" or list them by name.

- **Don't promise future providers.** Unless a new provider is confirmed for the current build, don't imply Pro includes tools beyond these 8.

- **Gemini works differently.** Gemini chats come through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download. It's fine to show a `gemini` subfolder in the Library in Option 1 or a "Gemini" puzzle piece in Option 3. Don't show or imply a one-click Gemini download.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time.** The fast subfolder pop-ins in Option 1 and the quick puzzle snaps in Option 3 are visual shorthand. Don't suggest all 8 providers download in a single click.

- **Viewers sign in to each provider locally.** "No account" means no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers inside the app.

- **Get the tiers right every time.**

  - **Free Tier:** ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each.
  - **Pro:** all 8 providers, unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Always label the price.** Say "$24 one-time" and label it as the 50% off launch price on screen. Update Option 2's on-screen text, the callback, the end card, the caption, and the pinned comment when the launch price ends.

- **Keep the Free Tier framing generous.** Option 2 calls the Free Tier a "sample" to show its size, not to belittle it. Don't imply the Free Tier is broken, limited in quality, or a bait-and-switch.

- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider logos or brand colors.** Provider names may appear in plain handwriting on puzzle pieces and clipboards, or as plain-text folder names in File Explorer. Never show official logos, icons, or interface screenshots.
- **Keep props unbranded.** The soup pot, spoon, ladle, clipboard, and puzzle should carry no manufacturer or company marks facing the camera.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Use a clean demo Library.** In Option 1, the Library folder should show only the 8 provider subfolders, with no personal folders or files nearby. Blur account names, email addresses, and anything personal in the File Explorer address bar or sidebar.
- **Use demo chats only.** If any subfolder opens on screen, show non-sensitive demo files with realistic titles, such as `2026-09-02-pricing-page-draft.md`.

### Pause Timing

- **Keep the roll call even.** In Option 1, read all 8 names in a steady, flat rhythm, about a third of a second each. Speeding up or slowing down breaks the joke. Hold the finished 8-folder view for at least half a second so viewers can count it.
- **Let the ladle rise slowly.** In Option 2, the slow lift is the punchline. A quick grab kills it.
- **Space the puzzle snaps.** In Option 3, give each of the five pieces its own distinct snap. Rushing them together blurs the "five missing" payoff.
- **Hold the beats.** Each hook builds to one reveal: the full roll call, the ladle, or the finished puzzle. Let the pause run slightly longer than feels comfortable.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track upgrade clicks on Option 2.** It leads with price, so it may hold slightly fewer viewers but send more people to totalrecalls.app. Compare link clicks and Pro upgrades alongside retention before you call a winner.
- **Compare Option 2 against Video 4's receipt hook.** Both put the $24 one-time price up front. If both win on clicks, price-forward hooks are a proven lever for the final two videos.
- **Watch provider mentions on Option 1.** Comments like "Finally, someone mentioned DeepSeek" or "Does it do Mistral?" show which lesser-known providers draw interest. Use those signals to pick topics for provider-specific guides.
- **Watch returning viewers on Option 3.** It pays off Video 3's shell game, so it may perform better with subscribers than cold audiences. If your analytics allow it, compare retention for returning and new viewers.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in Videos 9 and 10.
