## Production Notes

- **Hook timing:** The first spoken word lands at 00:00. Skip the logo sting, intro music, and fade-in.

- **Delivery:** Keep it honest and unhurried. The founder should sound fine with viewers staying on Free. That relaxed confidence builds more trust than any push to upgrade. Say the price plainly, without a pause for effect.

- **Claim accuracy (critical):** This video states exactly what buyers receive, so every line must match the current build. Before recording, confirm:

  - The exact Free Tier allowance, such as provider count, conversation limits, or feature limits. Fill in \[FREE TIER ALLOWANCE] with that wording.
  - The exact Pro unlocks. Fill in \[PRO UNLOCKS] and the difference rows in the comparison table. Limit it to two or three items in the VO. Longer lists belong in the description.
  - Whether Pro includes future updates, and for how long. If it does, add it on screen. If it's uncertain, leave it out.
  - That the Free Tier truly requires no credit card and no sign-up.
  - That the "$24 one-time" price and "50% off" framing match the live checkout page exactly.

- **Fairness in the comparison:** Show the Free Tier doing real work on screen. Don't make it look broken or crippled to push Pro. Viewers who feel respected on Free are far more likely to upgrade later.

- **Caption style:** Use bold white text with a subtle dark outline, synced to the VO and placed in the lower third. Tier labels sit as upper-left chips so they never overlap captions.

- **Comparison table design:** Use the same two-card layout in Scenes 2 and 5 so viewers recognize it on return. Keep the shared rows visually identical across both columns. Only the difference rows should draw the eye. Hold the full table on screen for at least four seconds.

- **Provider safety:** Never show a real provider's interface, sign-in page, settings, or logos. Blur the sign-in step. Only the TotalRecalls app, Windows folders, and motion graphics should appear sharp.

- **Privacy on screen:** Record with a demo account holding only non-sensitive chats. Blur email addresses, account names, and any personal details in file names and previews.

- **Pacing:** Scenes 3 and 4 should feel balanced, at roughly the same length, so neither tier feels rushed or oversold.

- **Continuity:** Match the folder layout, file names, window chrome, and founder setup (wardrobe, lighting, background) used across the TotalRecalls series, especially Videos 4, 5, and 8.

- **Color arc:** Neutral in Scenes 1–3, warming slightly in Scene 4 and holding steady through the CTA. The grade should feel calm and even, not dramatic.

- **Playlists:** Add the video to "Start Here: Own Your AI Conversations" and "Privacy, Safety & Local-First Explained."

### Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Keep the founder story true.** Founder hooks only work if they're honest. Use Option 1 only if losing a needed chat was a real part of the origin. Use Option 3's "Tuesday. Mildly annoyed." as comic framing, and swap in real details if you prefer. Don't invent timelines, dramatic losses, or backstory beats that didn't happen.

- **Don't overstate the loss.** In Option 1, "lost an AI chat" can mean a closed tab, a chat you couldn't find, or history you couldn't reach. Don't claim a provider deleted your data unless that's exactly what happened, and don't name a provider as the cause.

- **Avoid "nobody else built this."** Option 2 says "Couldn't find one I liked," which is a personal opinion. Don't say "it didn't exist," "the only tool," or anything that makes a claim about competitors.

- **Wishlist items must match the product.**

  - **Local files:** chats are saved to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files.
  - **No account:** there's no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
  - **No subscription:** Pro is $24 one-time, labeled as the 50% off launch price.

- **Downloads still need internet.** Opening saved files works offline. Downloading from a provider needs a connection.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time.** Gemini comes through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download.

- **Get the tiers right in the CTA.** Free Tier: ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each. Pro: all 8 providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen), unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Wishlist pages, photos, and props must be unbranded. The laptop in Option 1 should show only TotalRecalls on a clean desktop.
- **Use a generic garage photo.** In Option 3, pick a stock-style image with no famous landmarks, company references, or recognizable people. Don't imply a comparison with any real company's origin story.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Check the background for personal details.** Founder story videos often feel more personal, so scan the frame for mail, family photos, sticky notes with passwords, or anything that reveals your address.
- **Don't recreate the "lost chat."** If Option 1 needs a visual of the lost chat, use a blank, generic chat bubble. Never show a real conversation.

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the stare in Option 1.** The unblinking pause before "the reasonable thing" is the setup. It should feel slightly too long.
- **Keep the wishlist rhythm even.** In Option 2, read each item at the same pace. The shrug needs a full beat before the three quick ticks.
- **Cut the choir hard.** In Option 3, the choir swell must stop the instant the photo leaves the founder's hand. A fade kills the joke.
- **Let the warmth show.** These hooks are more personal than earlier ones. A small, genuine half-smile at the end of each line is fine. Don't let it tip into a grin.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track subscribes, not just views.** Founder stories tend to earn follows more than clicks. Compare new subscribers per 1,000 views across versions as a key signal.
- **Watch for trust comments.** Replies like "Love that it's a solo dev" or "Is this just you?" show the story is landing. Pin a short, honest reply about who builds TotalRecalls and how you handle feedback.
- **Reuse what the comments teach you.** Founder stories are a natural fit for community spaces where authenticity matters. Note which hook draws the most genuine replies, and use that framing when you share the story elsewhere.
- **Compare against Videos 1–8.** Earlier tests weighed prop comedy, self-mocking humor, proof-first demos, and price-forward hooks. If a personal, story-first hook wins here, bring some of that honesty into the final video.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Use the winning approach as the starting point for the Video 10 hooks.
