## Why I Built TotalRecalls


I spent more than 45 years in financial services, most of it as an investment banker and venture capitalist. I advised companies, sat on boards, and watched a good number of startups rise and fall. The one thing I never pictured myself doing in retirement was writing software. Yet here I am, and a lost conversation is the reason why.
Let me explain.
The conversation I couldn't get back
Not long ago, I was deep into a research project with an AI assistant. Over several weeks I'd built up a thread that genuinely mattered — sharp questions, refined answers, a whole line of reasoning I'd shaped through dozens of back-and-forth exchanges. It was, in a real sense, my thinking laid out in front of me.
Then I went looking for it one morning, and it was gone.
A session had expired. Or maybe the interface had changed. Honestly, I still don't know which. What I do know is that weeks of careful work vanished with no warning and no way to get it back. There was no undo button, no support line, no quiet backup sitting on a server somewhere waiting for me to ask.
I sat there feeling something I hadn't felt in a long time: plain foolish. I'd spent an entire career telling people to protect their assets. And I'd let some of my most useful work live entirely inside a tool I didn't control.
It turned out I wasn't alone
Once I started paying attention, I saw the same story everywhere. A friend lost a debugging session when a tab closed at the wrong moment. A colleague couldn't find a conversation from three months earlier because the history had quietly rolled off. People running ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and three or four others, with their best ideas scattered across separate accounts — each with its own login, its own rules, and its own way of losing things.
Chat platforms simply weren't built to be permanent archives. They're built to answer you right now, not to promise you can find that answer next year. That isn't a failing of any one company. It's just what these tools are.
But it struck me as a real problem hiding in plain sight. We back up our photos. We keep copies of our tax records. Somehow our AI conversations — more and more, a record of how we research, write, and solve problems — were living somewhere we didn't own, with no copy anywhere else.
Why I decided to build it myself
I'm not a software company. For most of my life I couldn't have written a working line of code. But AI tools have matured to the point where a motivated person can actually build something useful. So I decided to find out if I was that person.
Two principles shaped every choice I made.
Local-first. Your conversations belong on your own machine, not inside another cloud service you're forced to trust. TotalRecalls downloads your chats from eight providers — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen — straight into a folder on your own computer. Everything runs locally. Your conversations never touch a server of mine, because there is no server of mine.
Privacy-first. There's no account to create and no sign-up. I don't collect your data, host your chats, or transmit your session tokens. I built it that way on purpose. After a career spent watching what happens when companies treat customer data as an asset to be mined, I wanted to make something that simply couldn't do that.
Your files land as plain Markdown and JSON — open, readable formats you can search, back up, or open in any tool you already use. No proprietary lock-in. If you ever walk away from TotalRecalls, your library still works. That mattered to me more than almost anything else.
Why one price, not a subscription
This part is personal too. I'm tired of subscriptions, and I suspect you are as well. The notion that you should pay every month, forever, just to keep access to your own conversations struck me as exactly the wrong model for a tool that's supposed to be about ownership.
So Pro is a one-time $24 — a discounted launch price, half off the regular rate. Not $24 a month. Not a fee you'll forget to cancel. You pay once, the license is yours, and you keep getting free updates to the core app for the life of the product.
I'd rather earn your trust with a fair, honest deal than trap you in a recurring bill. There's no outside funding pushing me toward growth at any cost. It's just me, which means I get to build the thing the way I believe it should be built.
Try it before you spend anything
I didn't want you to take my word for any of this, so there's a Free Tier. Download the app and run it on your own real conversations — no purchase, no commitment. It covers three providers with up to five conversations per download, which is plenty to see how it works before you decide a single thing.
I built TotalRecalls because I lost something I couldn't get back, and I didn't want that to keep happening — to me or to anyone else. If you've ever felt that same sinking feeling of a conversation gone for good, I think you'll understand exactly why it exists.
Try the Free Tier today at totalrecalls.app, and start keeping the conversations you'll want later.
— The founder, TotalRecalls
