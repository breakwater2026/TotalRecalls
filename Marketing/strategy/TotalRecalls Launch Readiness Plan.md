TotalRecalls Launch Readiness Plan
This is the companion to the gap-closing checklist. Where the checklist tells you what's still open, this plan tells you how to run the launch — day by day, channel by channel, asset by asset. It turns everything you've created (guides, video, headlines, ads, email sequences, blog posts, social, website copy) into a single execution sequence.
Who this is for: anyone owning the TotalRecalls launch — deciding what ships, when, to whom, and how you'll know it worked.
What you'll get:
Infrastructure you must have live before Day 1
An audience segmentation map to route the right message to the right person
A phase-by-phase execution plan (Phases 1–3) with channels, assets, CTAs, and timing
A Phase 3 retargeting plan
A KPI framework with per-phase metrics and a review cadence
The product itself stays simple throughout: TotalRecalls downloads your AI conversations from eight providers into a private local folder as Markdown and JSON. Pro is a one-time $24, and a Free Tier lets people try before they buy. Every message in this plan points back to that.

Retargeting pixels installed on homepage, pricing, and download pages
Mini-summary: If the blog isn't live, the list isn't collecting, and the tags aren't firing, hold the launch. These three unlock every phase that follows.
Audience segmentation map
Three buckets carry the whole campaign. Every asset should be aimed at one of them, and people should move between buckets as they act.
Bucket A — Prospects
People who subscribed for updates or guides but haven't downloaded anything.
Mindset: curious, still evaluating the "why."
Primary CTA: Try the Free Tier.
Message focus: loss aversion, ownership, category education.
Bucket B — Free-tier users
People who downloaded and tested the app but haven't upgraded to Pro.
Mindset: convinced it works, not yet convinced to pay.
Primary CTA: Upgrade to Pro — $24 one-time.
Message focus: the 5 locked providers, unlimited downloads, one-time price vs. subscriptions.
Bucket C — Buyers
People who purchased Pro.
Mindset: committed customer.
Primary CTA: none commercial — no re-selling.
Message focus: onboarding, tips, new-feature and new-provider updates. Exclude from all urgency and retargeting sell messages.
Movement rules: A prospect who downloads becomes B. A free-tier user who buys becomes C. Suppress buyers (C) from every upgrade and urgency send.

Channel and asset notes
Each spotlight links to its live guide and reuses the headline set for titles and social hooks.
Pair every spotlight with a short-form video cut from the primary launch video for social reach.
Add a "Related guides" cross-link block to each post — most users run more than one assistant, so this drives internal depth.
Email activation
Send a lightweight "new guide live" note to Prospects roughly weekly, batching two spotlights per send to avoid fatigue.
For Free-tier users, tie each spotlight to a locked provider: "DeepSeek is one of the 5 providers Pro unlocks." This is a soft upgrade nudge, not a hard sell.
Ad activation
Run low-budget, high-intent search/social ads against provider-specific terms (e.g., "download ChatGPT conversations").
Keep retargeting pools growing from spotlight traffic for the Phase 3 push.
Audience targeting notes
Spotlight visitors arrive with bottom-of-funnel intent — they're searching how to save a specific provider's chats. Put the free-tier CTA near the top, not buried at the end. Keep the technical depth intact; it's what ranks and what these readers want.
Mini-summary: Phase 2 is your SEO engine. Match each provider to its search intent, convert with an early free-tier CTA, and quietly grow the audiences Phase 3 will convert.

Phase 3 retargeting plan
This is where your Phase 1–2 audience-building pays off.
Retargeting audiences to activate:
Site visitors who viewed the pricing or buy page but didn't purchase
Free-tier download-page visitors and confirmed free-tier users
Video viewers (25%+ watched) from the launch and spotlight cuts
Guide-page readers from Phase 2
Exclusions (critical):
Suppress all buyers (Bucket C) from every retargeting ad and urgency email.
Suppress anyone who purchases mid-sequence — sync the buyer tag to your ad platform daily so paying customers stop seeing sell ads.
Creative and cadence:
Lead with the deadline and the one-time-vs-subscription math.
Reuse ad urgency copy already created; mirror the email subject lines for consistency.
Frame around the 5 locked providers for free-tier users specifically.
Email activation
Three-email countdown over Days 42–45 (heads-up → 48 hours → last call).
Send to Free-tier users first (highest intent), then Prospects.
Hard-exclude buyers. Remove anyone who converts between sends.
Audience targeting notes
Free-tier users have already seen the product work — you don't need to re-sell the concept, only the deadline. Prospects still need the value reminder folded into the urgency. Keep both honest: the price is a genuine launch discount, so state clearly what happens when it ends.
Mini-summary: Phase 3 is a tight, time-boxed conversion sprint. Point everything at warm free-tier users, exclude buyers cleanly, and let the deadline do the work.

Review cadence
Week 1 (end of Phase 1): Are downloads and signups tracking to target? Adjust ad creative and spend if cost per download is high.
Week 3 (mid Phase 2): Which spotlights are ranking and converting? Double down on winners; refresh underperformers.
Week 6 (end of Phase 3): Final conversion review. Did the discount hit its revenue target? Decide whether to extend, close, or step the price up as planned.
Standing rule: check the buyer-exclusion sync daily during Phase 3 so no customer sees a sell message after purchasing.
Common pitfalls to avoid
Launching before infrastructure is live. No blog or list means Phase 1 assets have nowhere to land and no one to nurture.
Selling Pro too early. Cold audiences need trust first — push the free download in Phases 1–2.
Fatiguing your list. Batch Phase 2 guide emails; don't send one per spotlight.
Re-selling to buyers. Without a daily buyer-exclusion sync, paying customers get urgency ads — the fastest way to annoy your best users.
Judging phases by the wrong metric. Awareness in Phase 1, search in Phase 2, conversion in Phase 3.
Next steps
Your remaining work is execution structure, not copywriting. To reach launch-ready, move in this order:
Stand up the infrastructure gate: live blog, email capture, segmentation, and tracking.
Load and schedule the Phase 1 assets against Days 1, 5, and 10.
Queue the seven Phase 2 spotlights with their short-form cuts and cross-links.
Build the Phase 3 retargeting audiences now, and set the buyer exclusions.
Wire the KPI dashboard so each phase reports against its own goal.
Clear those five, and every asset you've built is either live or scheduled — turning a folder of content into a running, measurable launch.
