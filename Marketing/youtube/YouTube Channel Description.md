Channel trailer script outline
Your channel trailer plays automatically for non-subscribers. It has one job: convince a first-time visitor to subscribe and try the app. Keep it under 60 seconds and front-load the hook.
Trailer structure (45–60 seconds)
[0:00–0:05] The hook
Visual: Dark screen, chat log lines failing and fading ("thread lost," "download failed").
Voiceover: "Every day, people lose months of AI conversations — and never realize it until it's too late."
Goal: Name the pain in the first three seconds.
[0:05–0:15] What TotalRecalls is
Visual: Logo forms as eight provider icons converge into a single folder.
Voiceover: "TotalRecalls is a Windows app that saves your AI conversations from eight providers into one private folder — on your own computer."
Goal: Explain the product clearly and quickly.
[0:15–0:28] The proof
Visual: Clean screen recording — a folder fills with Markdown and JSON files in real time.
Voiceover: "No cloud. No account. No subscription. Just files you own, saved locally, forever."
Goal: Show the product working to build instant trust.
[0:28–0:40] Who built it and why
Visual: Founder to camera, warm and direct.
Voiceover: "I built this because I couldn't get my own AI conversations back. After 45 years in finance, I wanted a tool that kept my data private and mine."
Goal: Establish credibility through the founder story.
[0:40–0:52] What the channel offers
Visual: Quick montage — demo clips, provider guides, privacy explainer titles.
Voiceover: "On this channel, I'll show you how to back up ChatGPT, Claude, and more — and how to keep every conversation that matters."
Goal: Give viewers a reason to subscribe.
[0:52–0:60] The close
Visual: URL and CTA card, price badge.
On-screen text / Voiceover: "Try it free. Pro is $24, one time. Windows app — visit from your desktop. Subscribe to keep your AI history."
Goal: Drive the subscribe and the click.
Trailer production notes
Keep the folder-filling screen recording identical to your other videos for instant recognition.
Frame founder shots consistently with your video series — same lighting, same background.
End on a static CTA card so the URL and price stay on screen long enough to read.
Takeaway: The trailer moves from pain to product to proof to person — then asks for the subscribe.
Keyword and SEO strategy for the channel page
YouTube reads your channel name, description, and video metadata to decide when to surface your content. A focused keyword strategy helps the right people find you through search and suggested feeds.
Primary keyword
"back up AI conversations" — this captures the core job your product does and matches high-intent search behavior.

Where to place keywords
Channel name / handle: keep it branded (TotalRecalls) but consider a descriptor in the banner.
About section: the primary keyword appears early and naturally, with provider names for relevance.
Video titles: lead with the keyword people actually type ("How to Export ChatGPT Conversations…").
Video descriptions: repeat the description template with the right supporting terms per video.
Playlist titles: use search-friendly names like "ChatGPT Backup" and "Own Your AI Data."
Search intent to target
Your strongest opportunities are problem-aware and solution-aware searches. Group your content around these intents:
Problem-aware: "I'm losing my AI chats" → loss-aversion and awareness videos.
Solution-aware: "how to export ChatGPT" → provider-specific walkthroughs.
Trust-checking: "is this app safe" → privacy and "100% local" explainers.
Takeaway: One primary keyword, a tight set of supporting terms, and placement that matches how people actually search.
Common mistakes to avoid
A few missteps can quietly undercut an otherwise strong channel page.
Vague About copy. "We help you manage your data" says nothing. Name the eight providers and the local-first promise.
Hiding the Windows-only fact. Mobile viewers deserve the "visit from desktop" nudge upfront, not a surprise later.
Keyword stuffing. Repeating "AI backup" ten times reads as spam and hurts trust. Use natural, varied phrasing.
A trailer that buries the hook. If you haven't named the pain by second three, most viewers are gone.
Inconsistent branding. A tagline in one place and a different one elsewhere weakens recognition.
Takeaway: Be specific, be honest about the platform, and stay consistent everywhere.
