The TotalRecalls YouTube Upload Checklist and Template
Publishing videos gets faster when you stop reinventing the process every time. If you're running TotalRecalls solo, a repeatable checklist turns each upload from a scramble into a routine. This is the second asset in your two-part YouTube strategy set, built to sit right beside your 10-video content plan.

Every CTA is written for one reality — TotalRecalls is a Windows-only desktop app, so mobile viewers always get a "visit from desktop" nudge.

How to use this checklist
Treat this as your master template. Copy it once per video, fill in the placeholders, and check off each item as you go. The goal is consistency: same structure, same CTA pattern, same finish across every upload.
Work through it in three phases — before upload, during upload, and after upload. Skipping the post-upload phase is the most common mistake, and it's where a lot of easy reach gets left on the table.
Takeaway: One filled-in copy per video keeps your channel consistent and your workflow fast.
Phase 1: Pre-production checklist
Before you touch YouTube Studio, lock down the four elements that decide whether a video gets watched. If any of these are weak, better settings won't save the upload.
Hook (first 3–5 seconds)
Your opening line does the heavy lifting. Write it before anything else.
Hook identifies the pain, shows the product working, or challenges an assumption
Hook is spoken (or shown on screen) within the first 3 seconds
Hook matches the title's promise — no bait-and-switch
Title
Write titles the way people actually search, not clever wordplay.
Primary keyword sits near the front (e.g., "Export ChatGPT Conversations…")
Title is clear, specific, and under ~60 characters where possible
Title reflects the video's real content and funnel stage
Thumbnail brief
You may not design it yourself, but you should brief it clearly.
Single focal idea (disappearing chats, "8 providers → 1 folder," "100% local," or "$24 one-time")
High contrast, minimal text (3–4 words max)
Readable at small mobile size
Consistent style with your other thumbnails
CTA
Every CTA respects the Windows-only reality.
CTA names the action: try the Free Tier, or upgrade to Pro
CTA includes the line "Windows app — visit from desktop"
Same CTA appears in the spoken close, description, and pinned comment
Takeaway: Hook, title, thumbnail, and CTA decide most of your results before upload even starts.
Phase 2: Upload settings checklist
Once the file is ready, work through YouTube Studio in order. These settings control how the video is found, understood, and navigated.
Core details
Title: final, keyword-forward version pasted in
Description: filled from the template below (placeholders replaced)
Thumbnail: custom upload, not an auto-generated frame
Playlist: assigned to the right group (see Phase 3)
Category and tags
Category: Science & Technology (or Education for walkthroughs)
Tags: primary keyword + 5–8 semantic variations (e.g., "backup ChatGPT," "save AI chat history," "local AI backup," provider names)
Language: English (US)
Audience and visibility
"Made for kids": set to No
Visibility: Scheduled or Public per your rollout week
Schedule time: set for consistent publishing day/time
End screens and cards
End screen: add on the last 5–20 seconds
One element linking to your website/CTA
One "next video" (usually the explainer or a provider walkthrough)
Subscribe element
Cards: add 1–2 mid-video cards pointing to a related walkthrough or the explainer
Takeaway: Fill settings top to bottom every time so nothing gets skipped under deadline pressure.
Phase 3: Post-upload actions
This phase is where solo operators gain the most reach for the least effort. Do these immediately after publishing, while the video is fresh.
On YouTube
Pinned comment: post your CTA with the "Windows app — visit from desktop" line
Playlist check: confirm the video landed in the correct playlist ("Start Here," "Product Demos," "ChatGPT Backup," etc.)
Reply readiness: watch early comments for objections you can answer (and turn into future videos)
Cross-posting
Repurpose each upload across your other channels the same day.
Cut 2–4 vertical Shorts from any long-form video
Post one LinkedIn update linking the video
Post one X update with the hook as the opener
Add a mention in your next email send
Embed the video in the matching blog post where relevant
Takeaway: The video isn't "done" at publish — it's done after cross-posting and the pinned CTA.
