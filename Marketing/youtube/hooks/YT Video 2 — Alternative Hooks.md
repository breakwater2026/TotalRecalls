# Video 2 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 2 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 2 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry, understated delivery used across the series.

Video 1 named the problem. These hooks move one step further and show what it feels like to *live* with that problem every day. They cover ownership, lost chats, and the messy workarounds people rely on.

## Hook Option 1: The Rental Agreement

**\[VO / Founder, calm and slightly conspiratorial]**\
You don't own your AI chats. You're renting them.\
**\[pause: one beat]**\
And the landlord sets the rules.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in word by word)\
YOU DON'T OWN YOUR AI CHATS.\
YOU'RE RENTING THEM.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. The founder holds up a single printed page labeled "RENTAL AGREEMENT" in large type. It's a generic prop with no provider names or logos. On "You're renting them," the founder taps the page. During the pause, the founder slowly flips the page toward the camera to reveal a wall of tiny, unreadable fine print. On "sets the rules," the founder lowers the page and raises one eyebrow.

**\[SFX]**\
A crisp paper rustle on the first line. Silence through the pause. A single rubber-stamp "thunk" on "rules," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
One metaphor reframes the whole issue. Most viewers have never thought of their chat history as something they don't control, and "renting" makes that idea click right away without any technical explanation.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder holding the same page, now with "OWNED" stamped across it in bold. A quick flip back to the blank front resets the frame to the opening pose.

## Hook Option 2: The Endless Scroll

**\[VO / Founder, dry and patient]**\
Somewhere down here is that brilliant chat from March.\
**\[pause: half a beat, scrolling]**\
Still scrolling. Still scrolling. I'll pack a lunch.

*(17 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (upper third, updates on each line)\
FINDING ONE OLD AI CHAT…\
STILL SCROLLING…\
EST. ARRIVAL: TUESDAY

**\[Visual]**\
Close-up of the founder's hand on a mouse, then a quick cut to a blurred, generic chat sidebar with no provider branding. The sidebar scrolls downward and speeds up with each "Still scrolling." Cut back to the founder, leaning back with chin on hand and looking mildly bored. On "I'll pack a lunch," the founder slides a brown paper lunch bag into frame and sets it beside the laptop without breaking eye contact.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft, continuous mouse-wheel whir that rises in pitch as the scroll speeds up. A paper-bag crinkle on "lunch." Hard cut to silence into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Almost everyone who uses AI tools has hunted for an old chat and given up. The deadpan escalation turns a mild frustration into a joke viewers recognize instantly. The lunch bag gives the opening a memorable visual beat in the first 5 seconds.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder reaching for the lunch bag, then pushing it back out of frame with a small smile. The scroll hand returns to the mouse, matching the opening shot.

## Hook Option 3: The Sticky Note System

**\[VO / Founder, proud and completely deadpan]**\
This is my AI chat backup system.\
**\[pause: one beat]**\
Sticky notes. Hundreds of them. It's going great.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
MY AI CHAT BACKUP SYSTEM:\
📝 📝 📝\
IT'S GOING GREAT.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, gesturing proudly at a monitor completely covered in colorful sticky notes. The notes have scribbled fragments like "that prompt?" and "the good one." On "Hundreds of them," the camera pulls back slightly to show notes spilling onto the desk, the lamp, and the founder's sleeve. On "It's going great," a single note peels off the monitor and flutters down. The founder watches it fall, then looks back at the camera, unbothered.

**\[SFX]**\
A cheerful "ta-da" sting on "backup system," cut off abruptly. Silence through the pause. A tiny paper-flutter sound as the note falls, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Self-mocking humor builds trust fast. The founder admits to the messy workaround many viewers secretly use, whether that's sticky notes, screenshots, or random text files. That makes the solution in the rest of the video feel earned, not pushy.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the monitor wiped clean except for one sticky note that reads "Saved." The founder gestures at it with the same proud pose from the opening frame.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

- **Keep the rest of the video unchanged.** Each hook fills only the opening \~5-second window, so Scene 2 onward stays as written. Read your chosen hook's last line aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

- **Adjust the Scene 6 callback.** Swap the callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

  - **Option 1:** "Now you own the place." (taps the "OWNED" page)
  - **Option 2:** "Lunch cancelled." (a keyword typed into Windows Search pulls up the old chat instantly)
  - **Option 3:** "Sticky notes: retired." (peels the last note off the monitor)

- **Hold the pauses.** The humor lives in the timing. Let each beat run slightly longer than feels comfortable.

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Any chat window, sidebar, or prop in the hook should be blank and generic. Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, and Windows Search should ever appear sharp on screen.

- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.

- **Keep props illustrative.** The "rental agreement" and sticky notes are comedic props. Don't imply that any specific provider will delete or restrict your chats.

- **Keep claims accurate.** If a hook or callback mentions saving chats, stick to what the product does. TotalRecalls saves chats to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds versus swiping away.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** For the full video, compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Give each version enough time.** Run each test for at least 7 days before judging, so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Compare against Video 1's winner.** If a hook style won in Video 1, note whether the same style wins again here. Patterns across videos are more reliable than a single result.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Once a hook wins, reuse its approach as the starting point for hooks in the rest of the series.
