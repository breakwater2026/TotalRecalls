# Video 3 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 3 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 3 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry, understated delivery used across the series.

Video 1 named the problem. Video 2 showed what it feels like to live with it. These hooks focus on the next layer: your AI work is scattered across several tools, with no single place that holds all of it. All three hooks name ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity. Those are the same three providers covered by the Free Tier, so the opening lines up with the offer at the end.

## Hook Option 1: The Group Project

**\[VO / Founder, dry and faintly weary]**\
Your best ideas are split across three AI tools. None of them talk.\
**\[pause: one beat]**\
Classic group project.

*(16 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
3 AI TOOLS.\
0 SHARED NOTES.\
CLASSIC GROUP PROJECT.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. The founder holds three index cards fanned out like a hand of playing cards. The cards read "CODE," "WRITING," and "RESEARCH" in plain marker, with no logos. On "None of them talk," the founder slowly turns the cards so each one faces away from the others. During the pause, the founder looks at the cards, then at the camera. On "Classic group project," the founder drops the cards flat on the desk with a tired half-smile.

**\[SFX]**\
A light card-shuffle flick on the first line. Silence through the pause. A soft, flat "thud" as the cards hit the desk, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Almost everyone has been stuck in a group project where nobody shares anything. That memory maps neatly onto using ChatGPT for one job, Claude for another, and Perplexity for a third. The joke lands in one line, and viewers instantly picture their own scattered setup.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the same three cards, now stacked neatly under a single fourth card labeled "LIBRARY." The founder fans them out again, matching the opening pose.

## Hook Option 2: The Missing Poster

**\[VO / Founder, solemn and completely deadpan]**\
Missing: one AI chat. Last seen three tools ago. Answers to "that good prompt."\
**\[pause: half a beat]**\
Reward offered.

*(16 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (styled like a photocopied poster, black on off-white)\
MISSING\
ONE AI CHAT\
LAST SEEN: 3 TOOLS AGO

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, standing beside a small corkboard. The founder pins up a hand-drawn "MISSING" poster. The "photo" is a sketch of a blank chat bubble with no provider branding. On "Answers to 'that good prompt,'" the founder taps the poster with one finger. On "Reward offered," the founder turns to the camera and holds up a single wrapped candy with total seriousness.

**\[SFX]**\
A thumbtack "click" as the poster goes up. A faint, lonely wind sound under the line. A tiny candy-wrapper crinkle on "Reward offered," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
It gives a common, low-stakes frustration an absurdly formal treatment, and the contrast is what makes it funny. "Last seen three tools ago" quietly makes the point of the video: when your work lives in several places, nothing is easy to track down.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder stamping "FOUND" across the poster, then pinning a fresh blank poster back up, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 3: The Shell Game

**\[VO / Founder, street-hustler patter, played flat]**\
Three AI tools. One brilliant answer. Which cup is it under?\
**\[pause: one beat, cups lift]**\
None. You closed the tab.

*(16 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in on the reveal)\
WHICH CUP?\
NONE.\
YOU CLOSED THE TAB.

**\[Visual]**\
Tight overhead or three-quarter shot of the desk. Three plain paper cups sit in a row, labeled "ChatGPT," "Claude," and "Perplexity" in simple handwritten text with no logos. The founder slides the cups around quickly, hustler-style. On "Which cup is it under?" the founder stops and gestures for the viewer to choose. During the pause, the founder lifts all three cups at once. Nothing is underneath. On "You closed the tab," cut to a medium close-up of the founder, who gives a slow, sympathetic nod.

**\[SFX]**\
Quick paper-cup scrapes on the desk during the shuffle. Silence as the founder waits for a guess. A deflating "womp" on "None," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
The shuffle pulls the eye in the first second, and the viewer tries to track the "right" cup, which builds attention. The twist that nothing is under any cup lands the real point: the answer wasn't lost in one tool. It was never saved anywhere you control.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the three cups lifted to reveal a small folded card under each one that reads "Saved." The founder sets the cups back down and resumes the shuffle, matching the opening shot.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Stick to what the product does.** TotalRecalls saves chats to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files. Don't imply it merges chats into one file, syncs between tools, or moves conversations from one AI to another.
- **"One place" means one local Library folder.** Chats land in provider subfolders inside a single Library on your PC. You can search them all with Windows Search. Keep callbacks and on-screen text within that description.
- **Downloads happen one provider at a time.** Don't suggest that all three tools download in a single click.
- **The named tools match the Free Tier.** ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity are all covered, at 5 conversations each. If the end card mentions all 8 providers, tie that to Pro at $24 one-time, labeled as the launch price.
- **"No account" means no TotalRecalls account.** Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
- **Keep numbers illustrative.** "Three tools ago" and similar lines are comic framing, not statistics.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Cups, cards, and posters may show provider names in plain handwriting, but never official logos, brand colors, or interface screenshots.
- **Keep chat visuals generic.** Any chat bubble, sidebar, or window in the hook should be blank and unbranded.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the beats.** The humor comes from timing. Let each pause run a little longer than feels comfortable.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Callback Adjustments

Swap the Scene 6 callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

- **Option 1:** "Group project: finally done." (stacks the cards under "LIBRARY")
- **Option 2:** "Found. No reward needed." (stamps "FOUND" on the poster)
- **Option 3:** "Now it's under every cup." (lifts a cup to reveal "Saved")

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Watch the comments on Option 2.** The poster format invites viewers to share their own lost-chat stories. Treat comment volume as a secondary signal alongside retention.
- **Compare against Videos 1 and 2.** If a hook style, such as prop comedy or deadpan self-mockery, keeps winning, lean on it for the rest of the series.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in later videos.
