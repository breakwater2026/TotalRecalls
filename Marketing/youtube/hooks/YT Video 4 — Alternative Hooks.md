# Video 4 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 4 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 4 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry, understated delivery used across the series.

The first three videos covered the core problem, the daily friction, and the mess of using several AI tools. Video 4 shifts from *the problem* to *the proof*. These hooks show what owning your chats looks like: files that open with Wi-Fi off, files you can carry in your pocket, and a price you pay once. The goal is to build trust by showing proof on screen instead of making claims.

## Hook Option 1: Pull the Plug

**\[VO / Founder, calm and quietly confident]**\
Don't take my word for it. Watch.\
**\[pause: one beat, Wi-Fi goes off]**\
Wi-Fi's off. My AI chats? Still here.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
WI-FI: OFF\
MY AI CHATS: STILL HERE

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. On "Watch," cut to a tight screen shot of the Windows taskbar. The cursor clicks the Wi-Fi icon and turns Wi-Fi off, and the "No internet" state is clearly visible. Quick cut to a Markdown file from the local Library folder opening cleanly in Notepad, showing a readable prompt and response. Cut back to the founder, who turns the laptop slightly toward the camera and raises one eyebrow.

**\[SFX]**\
A crisp click on the Wi-Fi toggle, followed by a soft "power-down" hum. Silence through the pause. A light, satisfied "tick" as the Notepad file opens, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Skeptical viewers don't trust words like "local." This hook skips the claim and shows the proof. Turning off Wi-Fi on camera is simple, easy to verify, and hard to argue with. It builds trust in 5 seconds.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder's cursor hovering over the Wi-Fi icon again and the laptop angled toward the camera, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 2: The Receipt

**\[VO / Founder, dry and faintly amused]**\
Another monthly subscription to keep your own chats? No.\
**\[pause: one beat, tiny receipt appears]**\
One receipt. Twenty-four dollars. Done.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in on the reveal)\
MONTHLY. MONTHLY. MONTHLY.\
→ ONE RECEIPT.\
$24 ONE-TIME · LAUNCH PRICE

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds. The founder unrolls a comically long paper receipt that spills off the desk and onto the floor. Every line reads "MONTHLY SUBSCRIPTION" in generic type, with no company names or prices. On "No," the founder lets the long receipt drop out of frame. During the pause, the founder holds up a tiny receipt between two fingers that reads "TotalRecalls Pro · $24 · One-time." On "Done," the founder tucks it into a shirt pocket.

**\[SFX]**\
A long, rattling receipt-printer whir under the first line. A soft paper "flump" as the long receipt hits the floor. A single, clean register "ding" on "Twenty-four dollars," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Subscription fatigue is real, and a physical receipt makes it visible in one image. The contrast between a long, endless receipt and a tiny one tells the whole value story without any math. This hook also brings the one-time price forward, which can attract viewers who care about cost.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder pulling the tiny receipt back out of the pocket and holding it up between two fingers. Then the founder picks up the long receipt again, matching the opening pose.

## Hook Option 3: The Pocket Test

**\[VO / Founder, matter-of-fact with a hint of a smirk]**\
My AI chats fit on this.\
**\[pause: one beat, holds up USB stick]**\
Plain files. Try that with a browser tab.

*(14 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
MY AI CHATS 👉 🔌\
PLAIN FILES.\
TRY THAT WITH A BROWSER TAB.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding a plain, unbranded USB stick at eye level. On "fit on this," quick cut to the screen: a Library folder of `.md` and `.json` files is dragged onto a removable drive in File Explorer, and the copy dialog finishes. Cut back to the founder, who pockets the USB stick. On "Try that with a browser tab," the founder gestures toward the laptop, which shows a blank, generic browser tab, and gives a small shrug.

**\[SFX]**\
The Windows "device connected" chime on the first line. A quick file-copy whoosh during the drag. Silence for half a beat after the shrug, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
"Local ownership" sounds abstract until you can hold it. A USB stick makes the idea physical: your chats are real files you can copy, back up, and carry. The contrast with a browser tab makes the difference between renting and owning feel obvious.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder pulling the USB stick back out of the pocket and holding it at eye level, matching the opening frame.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Offline means opening saved files, not downloading.** In Option 1, show only an already-saved Markdown file opening with Wi-Fi off. Downloading chats from a provider still needs an internet connection, so never show or imply offline downloads.
- **The USB copy is done by the user, not the app.** In Option 3, the drag-and-drop happens in File Explorer. Don't imply that TotalRecalls exports straight to USB or syncs anywhere. It saves chats to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files, and you can copy those files wherever you like.
- **Always label the price correctly.** Say "$24 one-time" and label it as the launch price on screen. Pro includes all 8 providers and unlimited downloads. Update the Option 2 receipt prop, on-screen text, and callback when the launch price ends.
- **Keep the subscription receipt generic.** The long receipt in Option 2 is a comedic prop. Don't name competitors, list real prices, or suggest that any provider charges to keep your chats.
- **"No account" means no TotalRecalls account.** Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
- **Free Tier stays consistent.** If the hook's energy carries into the CTA, the Free Tier is ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each.
- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Browser tabs, chat windows, and props must be blank and unbranded.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp.** The Windows taskbar Wi-Fi icon in Option 1 is fine to show.
- **Use demo files only.** Library folders and Markdown files on screen should use non-sensitive demo chats with realistic titles, such as `2026-08-14-launch-checklist.md`.
- **Blur personal details.** Hide account names, email addresses, network names, and the removable drive's label if it shows anything personal.
- **Use an unbranded USB stick.** No manufacturer logos facing the camera.

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the beats.** The humor and the proof both depend on timing. Let each pause run slightly longer than feels comfortable.
- **Give the proof moment room.** In Option 1, the Wi-Fi-off state and the opened file each need at least half a second on screen so viewers can read them. Don't rush the reveal.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Callback Adjustments

Swap the Scene 6 callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

- **Option 1:** "Still offline. Still here." (taps the Wi-Fi icon, still off)
- **Option 2:** "Receipt count: one." (pats the shirt pocket)
- **Option 3:** "Pocket-sized. All yours." (pockets the USB stick)

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track clicks for Option 2.** Because it leads with price, it may hold fewer viewers but send more people to totalrecalls.app. Compare link clicks alongside retention before you call a winner.
- **Watch for skeptical comments on Option 1.** Questions like "Is it really offline?" are a good sign. Pin a short, honest reply that explains what works offline and what needs a connection.
- **Compare against Videos 1–3.** Earlier hooks leaned on prop comedy and self-mocking humor. If a proof-first hook wins here, that suggests viewers respond to demonstrations, and later hooks in the series can lean more on showing than joking.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in Videos 5 through 10.
