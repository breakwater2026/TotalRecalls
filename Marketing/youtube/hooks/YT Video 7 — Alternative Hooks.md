# Video 7 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 7 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 7 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry delivery used across the series.

Videos 1 through 5 covered the core problem, the daily friction, the multi-tool mess, proof of ownership, and privacy with no account. Video 7 moves from *keeping* your chats to *using* them. These hooks focus on what happens after the download: finding an old chat fast, not asking the same question twice, and going back to your best work whenever you need it. Saved chats should be a working library, not a storage closet.

## Hook Option 1: The Needle Test

**\[VO / Founder, calm and quietly confident]**\
Everyone says finding an old AI chat is a needle in a haystack.\
**\[pause: one beat, keyword typed]**\
Watch. One word. Found.

*(17 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
NEEDLE IN A HAYSTACK?\
ONE WORD.\
FOUND.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. A small handful of craft straw sits in a pile beside the laptop. On "haystack," the founder pokes the straw with one finger, faintly skeptical. On "Watch," cut to a tight screen shot: a single keyword goes into Windows Search, and a Markdown file from the local Library folder appears as the top result. The file opens cleanly in Notepad. Cut back to the founder, who brushes the straw off the desk with the back of one hand.

**\[SFX]**\
A dry straw rustle on the first line. Soft keyboard taps during the pause. A crisp "ping" as the search result appears, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Viewers have heard "searchable" before, so the hook shows the search instead of describing it. One keyword and one result make a clean before-and-after in 5 seconds. It also builds on the proof-first approach from Video 4, which gives returning viewers a familiar kind of payoff.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder sweeping the straw back into a small pile and poking it once, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 2: The Tally Marks

**\[VO / Founder, dry and faintly weary]**\
Asking your AI the same question you asked last month?\
**\[pause: one beat, fourth tally drawn]**\
Fourth time. Still nowhere saved.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (upper third, beside the whiteboard)\
SAME QUESTION.\
ASKED: ||||\
SAVED: 0

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, standing next to a small whiteboard. The board has a plain heading, "SAME QUESTION AGAIN," and three tally marks. During the pause, the founder uncaps a marker and slowly draws a fourth tally without looking away from the camera. On "Still nowhere saved," the founder caps the marker, taps the board once, and gives a small, resigned shrug.

**\[SFX]**\
A long, squeaky marker stroke on the fourth tally. A soft cap "click." Silence for half a beat after the shrug, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Most people who use AI tools have re-asked a question because finding the first answer felt harder than starting over. The tally board turns that quiet habit into something viewers can count. The empty "Saved" line sets up the rest of the video, which shows the answer sitting in a local file.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder wiping the tally marks off the board in one swipe, then uncapping the marker again, matching the opening pose.

## Hook Option 3: The Recipe Box

**\[VO / Founder, warm but deadpan]**\
My best AI chats, filed like Grandma's recipes.\
**\[pause: one beat, pulls a card]**\
Pick any card. Cook it up again.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (styled like a handwritten recipe card, dark ink on cream)\
MY BEST AI CHATS\
FILED LIKE GRANDMA'S RECIPES\
PICK ANY CARD.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, with a small wooden recipe box open on the desk. The box holds plain index cards with handwritten tabs such as "Code Fixes," "Emails," and "Trip Plans." No provider names or logos appear. During the pause, the founder flips through the tabs and pulls out one card with a small flourish. On "Cook it up again," quick cut to the screen: the matching Markdown file opens in Notepad and scrolls to a readable prompt and response. Cut back to the founder, who tucks the card behind one ear like a pencil.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft wooden lid creak on the first line. Quick card-flip riffles in the pause. A gentle kitchen-timer "ding" on "again," then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
A recipe box is something you go back to, not something you store and forget. That one image reframes saved chats as tools you reuse. The warmth of the prop also balances the drier jokes from earlier videos, which gives the series some range.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder sliding the card back into the box and flipping through the tabs again, matching the opening shot.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Search runs through Windows Search, not a built-in search bar.** In Option 1, show Windows Search finding a file in the local Library folder. Don't imply that TotalRecalls has its own search engine unless the current build includes one.
- **Test indexing before you record.** Make sure the Library folder is included in Windows Search indexing. Confirm the keyword returns the right file as the top result on the first try. If it doesn't, choose a more distinct keyword. Don't fake the result with a jump cut.
- **No automatic tagging or categories.** The recipe tabs in Option 3 are a comic prop. TotalRecalls saves chats in provider subfolders inside one Library folder. Don't suggest it sorts chats by topic, adds tags, or builds collections.
- **Don't make claims about AI memory.** Option 2 is about the viewer's own habit of re-asking questions. Don't claim that any provider "forgets" chats, deletes history, or lacks memory features.
- **"Cook it up again" means reopening, not rerunning.** Saved files let you read and reuse past prompts and responses. Don't imply that TotalRecalls re-sends prompts to an AI or continues a chat.
- **Opening files works offline. Downloading doesn't.** If any hook shows Wi-Fi status, keep that distinction clear.
- **"No account" means no TotalRecalls account.** Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
- **Pricing and tiers stay consistent.** Free Tier: ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each. Pro: all 8 providers, unlimited downloads, $24 one-time, labeled as the launch price.
- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Whiteboards, recipe cards, and props must be unbranded. Handwritten topic labels are fine.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Use demo chats only.** Search results and opened files should use non-sensitive demo content with realistic titles, such as `2026-06-03-api-error-fix.md`.
- **Check search results for personal files.** Windows Search may show other documents from your PC. Use a clean demo user profile, or crop and blur anything outside the Library folder.
- **Keep the straw contained.** In Option 1, use a small, tidy pile so it doesn't clutter later founder shots or break continuity.

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the beats.** Each hook builds to one reveal: the search result, the fourth tally, or the pulled card. Let the pause run slightly longer than feels comfortable.
- **Give the proof room in Option 1.** Hold the search result and the opened file for at least half a second each so viewers can read them.
- **Draw the tally slowly in Option 2.** The squeak and the eye contact carry the joke. A fast stroke kills it.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track comments on Option 2.** A prompt like "How many times have you asked the same question?" in the pinned comment can turn the tally joke into replies. Treat comment volume as a secondary signal.
- **Compare Option 1 against Video 4's winner.** If proof-first hooks won there, check whether a search demo wins here too. Two wins in a row point to a clear pattern for Videos 8 through 10.
- **Watch for the tone shift in Option 3.** The recipe box is warmer than earlier hooks. If it holds viewers as well as the drier options, use warmth alongside deadpan humor in the final videos.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in Videos 8 through 10.
