# Video 9 Alternative Hooks

**Series position:** Video 9 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 9 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry delivery used across the series.

Videos 1 through 8 named the problem, proved ownership, protected privacy, put saved chats to work, and showed all 8 providers in one local Library. Video 9 steps out from behind the product and answers a simpler question: *why does this exist?* These hooks tell the founder story in miniature. There's one frustration, one wishlist, and one very ordinary desk where a solo builder decided to fix it. The humor comes from honesty and understatement, not from polish.

## Hook Option 1: The Overreaction

**\[VO / Founder, calm and completely matter-of-fact]**\
I lost an AI chat I really needed.\
**\[pause: one beat]**\
So I did the reasonable thing. I built an app.

*(17 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
LOST: 1 AI CHAT.\
REASONABLE RESPONSE:\
BUILD AN ENTIRE APP.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds, hands folded, expression neutral. On "really needed," the founder gives a small, pained wince. During the pause, the founder stares at the camera without blinking. On "the reasonable thing," the founder turns the laptop toward the camera. The screen shows the TotalRecalls app open on a clean desktop. On "I built an app," the founder turns back with a straight face and the faintest nod, as if this were the obvious choice.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft, sad "womp" on "really needed." Silence through the pause. A small, bright "ta-da" sting on "I built an app," cut off after half a second, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
The gap between the problem (one lost chat) and the response (a whole app) is funny on its own. It also tells the founder story in two lines. Viewers see a real person with a real frustration, not a company with a pitch. Self-mocking honesty builds trust fast, especially with skeptical viewers who have seen too many polished launch videos.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder turning the laptop back toward themselves and folding their hands again, matching the opening pose.

## Hook Option 2: The Wishlist

**\[VO / Founder, dry and methodical, like reading a grocery list]**\
My wishlist: local files. No account. No subscription.\
**\[pause: one beat, pen taps the list]**\
Couldn't find one I liked. So I built it.

*(17 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (styled like a handwritten list, dark ink on lined paper)\
MY WISHLIST:\
☐ LOCAL FILES\
☐ NO ACCOUNT\
☐ NO SUBSCRIPTION\
→ SO I BUILT IT.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding a single sheet of lined notebook paper. Three items are written in plain marker with empty checkboxes beside them. The founder reads each item in a steady rhythm, tapping the pen beside each line. During the pause, the founder looks up and gives a small, resigned shrug. On "So I built it," the founder ticks all three boxes in three quick strokes, then holds the list up to the camera.

**\[SFX]**\
A light pen tap on each wishlist item. Silence through the shrug. Three quick, satisfying pen "scratches" as the boxes get ticked, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
It turns the product's core promises into personal standards. The founder didn't start with features. They started with a list of things they wanted for themselves. Returning viewers will recognize each item from earlier videos, so the hook ties the whole series back to one person's reasons. The ticked boxes give a clean, visual payoff in 5 seconds.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder flipping to a fresh page with the same three unticked items, pen hovering, matching the opening frame.

## Hook Option 3: Not a Garage

**\[VO / Founder, dry and faintly amused]**\
Big tech stories start in garages.\
**\[pause: one beat, photo tossed]**\
This one started at a desk. Tuesday. Mildly annoyed.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in line by line)\
TECH LEGEND ORIGIN: A GARAGE.\
THIS ONE: A DESK.\
TUESDAY. MILDLY ANNOYED.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up, founder framed in the upper two-thirds, holding up a printed photo of a generic, unbranded garage. On "start in garages," the founder studies the photo with mock reverence. During the pause, the founder tosses the photo over one shoulder without looking. On "This one started at a desk," the founder pats the desk twice. On "Mildly annoyed," the founder gives the camera a flat, slightly grumpy look that softens into a half-smile.

**\[SFX]**\
A faint, inspirational "ahh" choir swell under the first line. It cuts off abruptly as the photo is tossed. A soft paper flutter as it lands off-screen. Two dull knocks on the desk pats, then a hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
It pokes fun at the dramatic founder myths viewers have heard a hundred times. The honest version, a regular person at a regular desk solving their own problem, feels more believable and more relatable. It also signals "solo builder" without saying it, which matters for viewers deciding whether to trust a small, independent Windows app.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder picking up another garage photo from the desk and holding it up with the same mock reverence, matching the opening shot.

## Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **Keep the founder story true.** Founder hooks only work if they're honest. Use Option 1 only if losing a needed chat was a real part of the origin. Use Option 3's "Tuesday. Mildly annoyed." as comic framing, and swap in real details if you prefer. Don't invent timelines, dramatic losses, or backstory beats that didn't happen.

- **Don't overstate the loss.** In Option 1, "lost an AI chat" can mean a closed tab, a chat you couldn't find, or history you couldn't reach. Don't claim a provider deleted your data unless that's exactly what happened, and don't name a provider as the cause.

- **Avoid "nobody else built this."** Option 2 says "Couldn't find one I liked," which is a personal opinion. Don't say "it didn't exist," "the only tool," or anything that makes a claim about competitors.

- **Wishlist items must match the product.**

  - **Local files:** chats are saved to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files.
  - **No account:** there's no TotalRecalls account. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally.
  - **No subscription:** Pro is $24 one-time, labeled as the 50% off launch price.

- **Downloads still need internet.** Opening saved files works offline. Downloading from a provider needs a connection.

- **Downloads happen one provider at a time.** Gemini comes through a Google Takeout-to-Markdown conversion, not a direct download.

- **Get the tiers right in the CTA.** Free Tier: ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each. Pro: all 8 providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen), unlimited downloads, $24 one-time.

- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Wishlist pages, photos, and props must be unbranded. The laptop in Option 1 should show only TotalRecalls on a clean desktop.
- **Use a generic garage photo.** In Option 3, pick a stock-style image with no famous landmarks, company references, or recognizable people. Don't imply a comparison with any real company's origin story.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage.
- **Check the background for personal details.** Founder story videos often feel more personal, so scan the frame for mail, family photos, sticky notes with passwords, or anything that reveals your address.
- **Don't recreate the "lost chat."** If Option 1 needs a visual of the lost chat, use a blank, generic chat bubble. Never show a real conversation.

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the stare in Option 1.** The unblinking pause before "the reasonable thing" is the setup. It should feel slightly too long.
- **Keep the wishlist rhythm even.** In Option 2, read each item at the same pace. The shrug needs a full beat before the three quick ticks.
- **Cut the choir hard.** In Option 3, the choir swell must stop the instant the photo leaves the founder's hand. A fade kills the joke.
- **Let the warmth show.** These hooks are more personal than earlier ones. A small, genuine half-smile at the end of each line is fine. Don't let it tip into a grin.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Callback Adjustments

Swap the Scene 6 callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

- **Option 1:** "Still think it was reasonable." (a small nod at the laptop)
- **Option 2:** "Wishlist: done." (holds up the fully ticked page)
- **Option 3:** "Still just a desk." (pats the desk twice)

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Track subscribes, not just views.** Founder stories tend to earn follows more than clicks. Compare new subscribers per 1,000 views across versions as a key signal.
- **Watch for trust comments.** Replies like "Love that it's a solo dev" or "Is this just you?" show the story is landing. Pin a short, honest reply about who builds TotalRecalls and how you handle feedback.
- **Reuse what the comments teach you.** Founder stories are a natural fit for community spaces where authenticity matters. Note which hook draws the most genuine replies, and use that framing when you share the story elsewhere.
- **Compare against Videos 1–8.** Earlier tests weighed prop comedy, self-mocking humor, proof-first demos, and price-forward hooks. If a personal, story-first hook wins here, bring some of that honesty into the final video.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Use the winning approach as the starting point for the Video 10 hooks.
