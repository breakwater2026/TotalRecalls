### Hook Option 1: The Vanishing Act

**\[VO / Founder, showman energy, played deadpan]**\
Watch closely. Your best AI chat… gone.\
**\[snap, pause: one beat]**\
Now watch me bring it back.

*(15 words)*

**\[On-screen text]** (center, bold white, pops in word by word)\
NOW YOU SEE IT.\
NOW YOU DON'T.

**\[Visual]**\
Medium close-up of the founder at the desk, framed in the upper two-thirds. The founder holds up a small card or tablet showing a blank, generic chat window with no provider branding. On "gone," the founder snaps, and the window vanishes in a quick puff-of-smoke graphic. The founder raises one eyebrow at the camera. On "bring it back," the founder cracks their knuckles, turns toward the laptop, and the shot hard cuts to Scene 2.

**\[SFX]**\
A soft drumroll under "Watch closely," then a crisp finger snap and a small "poof" on "gone." Silence through the pause. A light "ta-da" sting on the hard cut into Scene 2.

**\[Why it works]**\
Motion and a clear before-and-after stop the scroll in the first second. The promise to "bring it back" creates an open loop, and the rest of the video closes that loop when the chat reappears as a local file.

**\[Loop note]**\
End the video with the founder holding up the same card, now showing the chat as a readable Markdown file. A quick snap resets the frame to the opening pose.

It makes the viewer do the thinking. Most people can't actually point to a folder on their computer that holds their AI chats, and that small moment of realization is more convincing than any explanation.

**\[Loop note]**\

### Production Notes for All Three Hooks

- **Keep the rest of the video unchanged.** Each hook fills the opening \~5-second window, so every scene after it stays as written. Make sure the last line of your chosen hook flows naturally into the first line of Scene 2.

- **Adjust the Scene 6 callback.** Swap the callback line so it echoes the hook you choose:

  - **Option 1:** "Told you I'd bring it back." (quick snap)
  - **Option 2:** "Now you can point to it." (points at the Library folder)
  - **Option 3:** "Local copies: all of them." (taps the notepad)

- **Hold the pauses.** The humor comes from timing. Let each beat feel slightly too long.

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Any chat window in the hook should be a blank, generic interface.

- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.

- **Keep the numbers illustrative.** The counters in Option 3 are for effect. Don't present them as real user statistics.

- **Keep claims accurate.** If a hook mentions saving chats, stick to what the product does: it saves chats to the viewer's own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching versus swipe away in the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at the 30-second mark.** For the full video, compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook should flatten the early drop-off.
- **Give each version enough time.** Let each test run for at least 7 days before judging, so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Once a hook wins, reuse its style as the starting point for hooks in the rest of the series.
