## Production Notes

- **Hook timing:** The first spoken word lands at 00:00. Skip the logo sting, intro music, and fade-in.
- **Caption style:** Use bold white text with a subtle dark outline, synced to the VO and placed in the lower third. Step labels sit as upper-left chips so they never overlap captions.
- **Provider safety:** Keep every depiction of Claude's interface, sidebar, or native export abstract or blurred. Only the TotalRecalls app and Windows folders should appear sharp.
- **Privacy on screen:** Record with a demo account that holds only non-sensitive chats. Blur email addresses, account names, and any personal details in file previews.
- **Proof moment prep:** Seed the demo account with a genuine, lengthy coding session so the search hit and code block look authentic. Choose a keyword that returns one clear result.
- **Pacing:** Keep Scenes 4–6 unhurried. Hold each step's key frame for at least one full second so viewers can follow along.
- **Continuity:** Match the folder layout, file names, window chrome, and founder setup (wardrobe, lighting, background) used across the TotalRecalls series.
- **Cards:** Add one mid-video card at about 00:47 pointing to the ChatGPT walkthrough (Video 4).
- **End screen:** Include the website link, the "Claude Backup Guides" playlist, and a subscribe element.
- **CTA consistency:** The line "Windows app — visit from your desktop" must appear exactly as written in the video, description, and pinned comment.

**Series position:** Video 5 of 10, Alternative Hooks\
**Replaces:** Scene 1, the opening \~5 seconds\
**Tone:** Wry, confident, lightly playful

Each hook below replaces the opening scene of Video 5 and hands off directly to Scene 2. Every option opens cold on the first spoken word, runs about 5 seconds, and keeps the dry, understated delivery used across the series.

The first four videos covered the core problem, the daily friction, the scattered multi-tool mess, and on-screen proof of ownership. Video 5 moves to privacy and control. These hooks focus on what TotalRecalls leaves out: no sign-up, no extra cloud, and no one else in the loop. The humor comes from removing steps and middlemen that viewers expect to see.

### Production Notes for All Three Hooks

### Claim Accuracy

- **"No account" means no TotalRecalls account.** There's no sign-up, email field, or password for the app itself. Viewers still sign in to their own AI providers locally to download chats. Option 1 must never suggest that provider logins disappear too.
- **"No cloud" means TotalRecalls adds no cloud of its own.** Chats are saved to your own Windows PC as Markdown and JSON files and aren't uploaded to a TotalRecalls server. Don't suggest the app removes chats from the provider. The originals still live with ChatGPT, Claude, and the other providers.
- **The guest list is about the local copy.** In Option 3, "Me" refers to the files on your PC. Don't claim the chats are invisible to providers, and avoid absolute words like "unhackable," "100% private," or "completely secure." Anyone with access to your PC can open the files, so a short reminder to lock your Windows account works well in a pinned comment.
- **Downloading still needs internet.** Opening saved files works offline. Downloading from a provider needs a connection. Don't blur the two.
- **Keep the cloud props generic.** Option 2 mocks "yet another cloud," not a specific company. Don't name competitors or imply that any service misuses data.
- **Pricing and tiers stay consistent.** Free Tier: ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity, 5 conversations each. Pro: all 8 providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Qwen), unlimited downloads, $24 one-time, labeled as the launch price.
- **Windows only.** TotalRecalls runs on Windows 10 and 11. Keep "Windows app — visit from your desktop" in the end card and caption.

### Visual Safety

- **No real provider screens or logos.** Sign-up forms, cloud props, and lists must be blank and unbranded. Hand-lettered labels like "CLOUD #1" are fine.
- **The sign-up form must be fictional.** Don't copy the layout, colors, or wording of any real provider's login or registration page.
- **Only TotalRecalls, Windows folders, Notepad, File Explorer, and Windows Search should appear sharp** in any screen footage that follows the hook.
- **No personal data on props.** Leave form fields empty or use obvious placeholders like "<name@example.com>."

### Pause Timing

- **Hold the beats.** Each hook builds to one reveal: the rip, the second cloud, or the empty list. Let the pause run slightly longer than feels comfortable.
- **Cut Option 1 mid-word.** "Verify your—" should stop abruptly, as if the founder lost patience. A clean interruption sells the joke.
- **Give the list reveal room.** In Option 3, hold the close-up of "1. ME" for at least half a second so viewers can read it.
- **No music for the first 2 seconds.** Let the VO and SFX carry the opening.
- **Cut hard into Scene 2.** No fades or transitions. Read the last line of your chosen hook aloud, then the first line of Scene 2, and make sure the handoff sounds natural.

### Split-Testing Guidance

- **Test one variable at a time.** Change only the hook. Keep the title, thumbnail, and every later scene identical.
- **Use Shorts cutdowns for quick reads.** Publish a short vertical cut of each hook version about a week apart. Compare how many viewers keep watching past the first 3 seconds.
- **Check long-form retention at 30 seconds.** Compare the audience retention curve at 0:30 across versions. A strong hook flattens the early drop-off.
- **Run each test for at least 7 days** so early spikes don't skew the result.
- **Expect privacy questions.** Comments like "But I still log in to ChatGPT" or "Is it really local?" are useful signals. Pin a short, honest reply that explains what stays on your PC, what still lives with the provider, and what needs a connection.
- **Watch returning viewers on Option 2.** It pays off Video 2's rental metaphor, so it may perform better with subscribers than with cold audiences. If your analytics allow it, compare retention for returning and new viewers.
- **Compare against Videos 1–4.** Earlier tests weighed prop comedy, self-mocking humor, and proof-first demos. If a "removal" hook, where the joke is what's missing, wins here, use that approach for the later trust-focused videos.
- **Carry the winner forward.** Reuse the winning approach as the starting point for hooks in Videos 6 through 10.
